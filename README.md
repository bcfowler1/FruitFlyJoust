# Fruit Fly Joust — The Ride

**Biological-reference toggle:** Simulation Controls offers FlyWire + FlyBody and Eon / NeuroMechFly. The latter launches the articulated WalkingLab with optional full-connectome steering, calibrated floor steering, and walking/perch/assisted-launch controls. This is our integration of public components, not Eon's complete released embodiment or validated biological control. Selection is saved; Apply restarts the matching backend.

**Continuous contact lab:** In Simulation Controls enable Detailed MuJoCo body, Six-claw contact experiment, Experimental approach / launch, and Continuous surface-relative cruise. Choose Wall or Ceiling and Apply mode and run. Reins steer engineering surface-relative cruise; stick back/forward changes separation from the surface. Hold B/L to approach and grip, A/Space to launch; Start/Backspace resets. Actual claws share one full-body physics session with engineering flight forces/yaw, fixed leg stance, an infinite flat surface and a 5 cm excursion limit. This is not wing-resolved or neural flight.

**Borrowed rider:** Silverspur riding, walking, sword and archery clips drive the humanoid in both the combat prototype and WalkingLab. Root motion is off; combat preserves mounted legs. Weapons attach to the hands; the lance points forward while mounted. Sword damage waits 0.18 simulation seconds for its windup. Thorax leg grip is an optional artistic IK pose. Exact anatomical fitting and mount/dismount choreography remain unvalidated. The source project is unchanged.

**Scientific-body combat:** In WalkingLab choose Floor and enable Walking / perch / assisted launch. Add combat practice in the Game view; Enemy combat switches between stationary targets and swordsmen/archers. C/X dismounts only from a verified claw hold with a safe nearby foothold. Dismount removes the actual one-third rider load; remount restores it. Sword, arrows, enemies and rider animations advance on the body clock and pause together. Damage and target collisions are gameplay rules outside MuJoCo. Fourteen checks passed with the brain off and fifteen with all 138639 neurons, nine scientific enemy checks passed, and eight clock/toggle checks passed. See Research/CURRENT_STATUS.md.

**Performance:** Cache repeated body observations preserves the full walking physics and gait. The measured speedup was 1.19× with exactly equal tested states and reset. It is enabled by default and can be toggled off. Detailed simulation remains slower than real time.

**Turning:** Both measured yaw-rate and heading candidates failed held-out error limits and are not enabled. See Research/turning-calibration-evaluation.json and averaged-heading-calibration.json. Rein/yaw effects remain engineering controls.

**Calibration and perch experiments:** Simulation Controls now offers Fast mode → Empirical translation lab when Detailed MuJoCo body is disabled. This real-time translation response was fitted to ten loaded flight references and tested on a separate reference (1.9 cm/s RMS velocity error, 4.2 cm/s maximum). Yaw and rider-cue behavior remain engineering approximations; the lab has fixed wings and no collisions, landing, combat, or live brain. The original Combat prototype remains a separate option. For actual contact transitions, enable Detailed MuJoCo body, Six-claw contact experiment, and Experimental approach / launch. B / L requests approach; A / Space launches; Start / Backspace resets airborne. The approach uses averaged external forces, then removes flight support after settled claw contact. These are flat-surface experiments with fixed leg targets, not validated wing-resolved or neural landing control.

Unity 2022.3.62f3 starter prototype. Open this folder in Unity Hub, or run OpenPrototype.ps1. On first import the Editor automatically generates and opens Assets/Practice.unity. Press Play.

**Plant lab:** Open Assets/PlantLab.unity for the combat encounter plus three editable, invented broad-leaf plants in faceted floor pots and a fourth hanging plant near a corner. The floor plants have buds, flowers, young and ripe fruit, and smaller tip leaves; the hanging pot has five trailing vines and a ceiling hanger. Select a plant and use its ProceduralPlant Inspector controls for stem facets, internodes, branching, leaf outline and posture, reproductive organs, and pot geometry; click Regenerate and save plant meshes. Turning off Include Pot places floor-plant growth at ground height for future outdoor scenes. Fruit Fly → Plants provides scene generation and contact validation. See Research/PLANT_GENERATOR.md for the variables, mathematical basis, and validation limits.

Open **Fruit Fly → Simulation Controls** for system toggles and live sliders. Disable Detailed MuJoCo body for the fast combat approximation. Enable it for trained physical flight; add Full connectome for experimental neural flight, or Six-claw contact experiment for wall/ceiling forces. Apply mode and run restarts the scene and resets the encounter. Neural wing influence scales the uncalibrated residual from zero to full; zero does not stop brain computation. Claw grip strength scales actual contact adhesion from zero to full. Fast mode is the existing gameplay approximation, not a validated reduction of the scientific model. The control panel currently runs in the Unity Editor.

The separate **Assets/FlightLab.unity** research scene streams the published trained FlyBody flight controller with a rider weighing one third of the fly. Enter Play, then Fruit Fly → Research → Start Trained Flight Backend. P pauses/resumes, Backspace replays. The separate Start Experimental Neural Flight Backend option adds real connectome spikes and bounded wing residuals through an explicitly unvalidated feedback mapping. See Research/UNITY_FLIGHT.md.

**Assets/ResearchLab.unity** can display six-claw wall/ceiling contact physics through Fruit Fly → Research → Start Six-Claw Wall Backend or Start Six-Claw Ceiling Backend. A / Space releases contact adhesion, B / L requests grip at existing contacts, Start / Backspace resets, and P pauses. This is a contact experiment, separate from the flight approach controller.

Left stick / WASD influences reins: left/right steers, pulling back / S requests a climb, pushing forward / W requests a dive. Vertical stick movement does not change cruising speed. Right stick / mouse controls independent look and aim. Press Space / A for takeoff and a speed burst that settles to a smaller cruising increase. Each press adds 2 speed units to cruise (maximum 13) and a temporary 5-unit burst that decays over about two seconds. Press L / B to reduce cruise by 2 and cancel any burst; hold it for 0.65 seconds to request landing. Holding A does not repeatedly stack bursts; release and press again for another cue. E/Q or RB/LB remains available for altitude. R / right stick click recenters the camera. Backspace / Start resets the course and speed. Click the Game view for mouse look; Escape releases the mouse.

Fly through the three gold hoops, circle the brown pillar, then land on the green table. The fly slows and turns away from obstacles. The camera remains upright while its body banks.

Landing and perching: hold B / L to approach a nearby floor, wall, ceiling, or slope. The fly rotates its feet toward the chosen surface and softens its approach. It checks its footprint and perches only when close, oriented correctly, and moving slowly. Release B before touchdown to cancel the approach. Once perched you may release B; the fly stays attached with its wings resting. Press A / Space to launch away from the surface and resume flight. If B remained held during launch, release and hold it again before another landing. Narrow edges without a supported footprint do not count as perch targets. The HUD shows Flying, Landing, Perched, or Launching, and identifies the supporting surface when perched. The camera smoothly follows wall/ceiling orientation.

This is geometric attachment, not a validated adhesion or biomechanical model. See SIMULATION_DIRECTION.md for the research-model migration.

**Assets/CombatLab.unity** adds practice targets and deliberate rider dismounting. C / X dismounts from a perch with a nearby safe foothold, or remounts nearby. F / Y switches between sword and bow on foot, or lance and bow when mounted. RT / left click strikes with the sword or couches the lance; a lance hit requires actual forward body motion. Hold LT / right click to draw the bow and release to shoot. On foot, use the left stick / WASD to walk and A / Space to jump. Borrowed combat animations accompany simple weapon visuals. This scene uses the gameplay fly; WalkingLab separately supports the same combat adapter on its scientific body.

Mounted rider rule: the rider holds onto the fly with his legs in every orientation. His visual transform stays attached to the body during banking, wall perching, and upside-down ceiling perching. Dismounting is deliberate, not triggered by orientation.

**Assets/CombatEncounter.unity** adds pursuing swordsmen and archers, enemy arrows, rider health/defeat, and Start / Backspace reset. Opponents stop attacking when defeated. It uses the gameplay fly. The optional WalkingLab encounter uses the scientific body's simulation clock.

Xbox-compatible controllers on Windows use the built-in XInput API, with automatic connection/disconnection detection. Sticks have continuous radial dead zones; look has a response curve for fine aim. Adjust reinDeadZone, lookDeadZone, lookResponse, and invertLookY on the Fruit fly's RiderInput component. Adjust horizontalSensitivity and verticalSensitivity on the Rider camera. Right and left triggers are read independently as primaryAction and secondaryAction, used for weapons in CombatLab. The HUD displays the input device and live stick values.

Other controllers fall back to Unity's legacy input manager with Xbox-style Windows mappings; they may need axis remapping in Project Settings → Input Manager. Keyboard and mouse remain available. ResearchLab runs the full connectome with passive body physics; connectome control of flight remains unimplemented.

After script changes, stop Play mode, focus Unity and allow import/compilation, then press Play and click the Game view. The HUD should say Xbox-compatible gamepad. Move either stick and check its on-screen values; press A to take off.

Architecture: RiderInput → FlySenses → FlyBrain → FlyIntent → FlyMotor. RiderCamera handles independent look. Replace PlaceholderBrain with a FlyBrain subclass for future neural integration. This is a gameplay placeholder, not a biologically validated simulation.

To regenerate the scene, use Fruit Fly → Generate Practice Scene (overwrites generated scene/materials). Batch scene generation: Unity.exe -batchmode -quit -projectPath <project> -executeMethod PrototypeBuilder.Generate -logFile <log>.
