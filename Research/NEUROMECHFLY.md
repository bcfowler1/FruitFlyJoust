# NeuroMechFly walking integration

In Unity, open **Fruit Fly > Simulation Controls**, select **Eon / NeuroMechFly**, choose the full-connectome toggle, neural steering influence, calibrated floor steering and optional walking/perch/assisted-launch controls, then **Apply walking mode and run**. WalkingLab includes optional gameplay combat and exact observation caching. FlyBody wing flight remains a separate backend.

This is an experimental integration of public components, not a reproduction of Eon's complete emulation. [Eon's description](https://eon.systems/updates/embodied-brain-emulation) describes 15 ms neural/body windows, hand-chosen sensory/output gains, NeuroMechFly walking controllers, DNa01/DNa02 steering and oDN1 forward drive. Our version uses the released [FlyWire v783 brain code](https://github.com/eonsystemspbc/fly-brain) at a3db62f9436074e485c0278290c2164ed6150808 and [FlyGym 1.1.0](https://github.com/NeLy-EPFL/flygym/tree/v1.1.0), the older Gymnasium API for NeuroMechFly v2. It does not integrate Eon's visual model, gustatory controller, grooming, feeding or oDN1 mapping.

## Actual loop

The full CUDA LIF network advances 150 steps of 0.1 ms. Annotated v783 DNa01/DNa02 left/right neurons provide actual population spikes per 15 ms window. Synthetic direct descending excitation encodes rider steering, measured body speed and contact-gated yaw correction; it is explicitly not a biological sensory encoder. An uncalibrated difference in spike rates modulates left/right gait drive. The upstream hybrid controller generates CPG stepping and contact-based stumbling/retraction corrections over 150 body steps. Forward drive and spur/brake responses are engineering mappings. Zero influence uses direct engineering steering while the entire brain still runs; switching the brain toggle off starts the body-only backend.

The rider adds one third of the original body's native scaled mass to the thorax, with a small isotropic engineering inertia. Dismount removes this load without resetting physical position, velocity or clock; remount restores it. Native mass values are reported without an unverified physical conversion. Geometry positions are converted from millimeters to meters. The animated rider stays attached to the thorax, with optional artistic leg IK and hand-attached weapons; these are not anatomical or muscle validation.

## Checks

`walking-evaluation.json`: 69 meshes/geometries exported, loaded gait remained finite and moved 14.6 mm over 1.5 simulated seconds; one-third load confirmed; full-brain/body windows matched and reset passed. `walking-stream-evaluation.json`: localhost pose transport, brain telemetry, pause of both clocks, reset to a new session at zero, and synchronized resume passed. Headless throughput was approximately 0.05 times real time; Unity performance may be lower. This is execution validation, not biological validation or calibrated steering accuracy.

`walking-steering-evaluation.json` verifies the sign of left/right drive using loaded 0.9 s physical rollouts. Rightward cues now increase left-leg drive, producing the appropriate Unity yaw. `unity-walking-evaluation.json` records actual selector launch with the brain on (138639 neurons) and off (zero neurons), matching clocks, .03 times real-time speed, and clean stop with both UDP ports free. The rider was enlarged 30%, lowered and moved forward onto the thorax ahead of the wings; this revised pose was visually inspected. Artistic leg IK improved foot contact in Play-mode floor/wall/ceiling orientation checks; exact anatomical seat fitting remains unvalidated.

## Steering and contact handover

The calibrated floor-steering toggle uses loaded physical rollouts and measured yaw feedback. The independent four-sample fit check gave RMS 0.087 rad/s and maximum 0.129 rad/s error; six closed-loop trials gave RMS 0.100 rad/s and maximum 0.180 rad/s. These are averaged gait-window measurements for drive 0.5–0.9, not biological validation or instantaneous accuracy. Spur/brake outside that range uses explicitly labelled raw engineering drive. See walking-calibration.json and walking-controller-evaluation.json.

Measured speed, yaw rate and contacting claws now return to the full-brain worker. Synthetic yaw correction reduced the tested descending population difference from 141.7 to 72.2 Hz. This verifies an effective feedback path; it remains direct engineering excitation, without a validated sensory encoder or rate-to-gait neural decoder.

The walking/perch/assisted-launch toggle keeps the same NeuroMechFly physics instance through gait, claw hold, external-force launch and return to walking. Floor, wall and ceiling each passed two loaded launch-land-hold cycles and resumed gait; all three are exposed. Grip uses native contact adhesion. Launch and approach use an external generalized wrench, with fixed leg targets; they do not use wing-driven or neural landing control. A contact-only verification interval precedes the perched state. This is a single flat surface with static surface-relative gravity, not traversal across a floor-wall corner. FlyBody wing flight remains a separate body/backend.

Controls: stick left/right steering; A adds a burst and smaller sustained drive, or launches while perched; B lowers drive, holding requests grip/landing; forward/back stick resumes gait while perched; P pauses; Backspace/Start resets. The grip slider changes actual adhesion live. Add combat practice and Enemy combat are optional floor encounters. C/X mounts/dismounts, F/Y selects weapons, RT attacks, and LT draws the bow. Combat and animation use simulation time. Fourteen scientific combat checks passed body-only and fifteen with the full brain; nine enemy and eight clock/toggle checks passed. Wing-flight integration remains experimental and separate. See CURRENT_STATUS.md.

Workspace runtime: work/neuromechfly-env. Exact dependency versions are saved in walking-environment-freeze.txt. Upstream Apache-2.0 license is retained in NEUROMECHFLY_LICENSE.txt. The brain remains in the separate existing research environment.
